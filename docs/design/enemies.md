# Enemies

The enemy roster is one coherent expedition expressed through eight tactical roles. Variants change
one visible property—armor, range, or equipment—rather than multiplying behaviors. Human enemies
can surrender when isolated, injured, or when their objective becomes impossible. Drones shut down.

| Role                | Purpose                                 | Visible intent                    | Counterplay                                      |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Retrieval officer   | Baseline movement and restraint         | Move, mark, then fire             | Cover, break line of sight, clear mark           |
| Brace carrier       | Mobile protection for a team            | Project a directional shield      | Flank, displace, disable projector               |
| Lock engineer       | Alters routes and operates stabilizers  | Place brace or channel a lock     | Interrupt, interact with device, force movement  |
| Surveyor            | Long-range information pressure         | Paint a lane one round early      | Leave lane, raise cover, obscure sensor          |
| Restraint drone     | Fast suppression and pursuit            | Tether a visible target           | Break tether by distance or one damage           |
| Linebreaker rig     | Slow heavy objective threat             | Charge a tile or breach an anchor | Redirect, immobilize, attack exposed rear module |
| Field coordinator   | Elite support and reinforcement control | Grant a second intent to one ally | Jam command, separate formation                  |
| Departure auxiliary | Local guide working with the expedition | Reveal shortcut and flank         | Close route, persuade when leader is absent      |

## Damage language

Safekeeping weapons communicate restraint: adhesive lines, concussive pulses, braces, and remote
locks. This is not harmless. Incapacitation can injure, detention is coercive, and machines can
cause lethal environmental failure. The visual language avoids gore without pretending violence
has no consequences.

## Boss structure

There are no bespoke giant bosses. Three missions use command encounters assembled from standard
systems:

- a linebreaker rig crossing a district while engineers open its path;
- Vale's stabilizer network, treated as linked objectives defended by ordinary units;
- Renn's command rig in the finale, which enhances visible intents until its relays are disabled.

This keeps tuning in the same rulespace as the rest of the game. Commanders may surrender or be
incapacitated; their health bars are not moral arguments.

## Encounter budget

A standard map fields six enemies initially and no more than four telegraphed reinforcements. No
more than five hostile roles appear in one mission. A new role receives a safe demonstration before
being combined with another new role.
