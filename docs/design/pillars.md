# Design Pillars

## Familiar tactics, strange place

The novelty belongs to the habitat and its people, not to an opaque ruleset. Movement, cover,
action points, telegraphed attacks, displacement, and objectives should be understandable without
experimentation. The Long Turn changes authored routes at known times; it is not a general-purpose
topology simulation.

## One home in depth

Nacre is not a sample platter of the entire Glass Frontier. The campaign visits the same sectors
before, during, and after transformation so familiarity acquires meaning. Every map must reveal a
practice, dependency, argument, or relationship specific to this place.

## Opponents remain people

The Safekeeping is wrong to impose rescue, but its fear is earned and its personnel are not
disposable monsters. Objectives prioritize access, delay, extraction, persuasion, and disabling
machinery. Most defeated people are incapacitated. Mercy changes tactical conditions rather than
serving as a morality score.

## Information replaces luck

No attack has a hidden hit roll. Damage, ranges, enemy intents, reinforcements, and scheduled map
shifts are visible before commitment. Difficulty comes from competing objectives and positional
consequences, not surprise arithmetic.

## Choice changes meaning, not production scale

Choices alter dialogue, voluntary departures, allied assistance, optional objectives, and the
epilogue. They do not create alternate campaigns or unique maps. The player can express a position
without multiplying the content budget.

## Hope is permitted, not guaranteed

The tone rejects fatalism and cruelty as maturity. It still permits anger, institutional failure,
injury, compromised choices, and people who cannot reconcile. Hope appears through practical
solidarity and preserved agency; it is a guide, not the plot's thesis.

## Finishability is a feature

Any proposed system must earn its implementation, tuning, content, interface, and testing costs.
If it does not deepen tactical decisions or this specific story, it is cut. A polished six-hour
game fulfills the promise better than an unfinished simulation.
