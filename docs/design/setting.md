# Setting and Factions

## Inherited frame

The Glass Frontier permits connected habitats with radically different physical rules. Millions
remain undiscovered, and life inside a hab need not resemble ordinary reality. Homeomorph uses
that freedom to explore one contact, not to explain the whole setting. The Bloom and Shear are not
required as causes, threats, or thematic centers.

## Nacre

Nacre is a densely inhabited hab built across layered civic shells. Every seventeen local years,
the Long Turn changes which surfaces and passages are adjacent. A school wall can become a bridge;
an orchard can open onto its winter reservoir; a familiar corridor can complete a district-wide
loop. Matter does not randomly mutate. Relationships between prepared thresholds change according
to patterns that residents have studied for generations.

The Long Turn takes three days. Turnward crews inspect thresholds, paint temporary routes, move
people with limited mobility, and brace spaces whose next relationship is uncertain. Most people
treat it as difficult civic work: consequential, occasionally dangerous, and inseparable from
home. This Turn is worse than forecast because Nacre's Assembly concealed evidence that several
old anchors are degrading.

For production, Nacre has four reusable environment families: Receiving, Civic Shell, Works, and
Turn Interior. Maps revisit these spaces in different adjacency states.

## The Turnward

The protagonists belong to Turnward Crew Seven, a municipal maintenance and transition team. They
are credible tactical actors because their work already involves route planning, rescue, cutting,
temporary construction, medical response, and coordinated movement. They are not prophesied,
military commandos, or representatives of the whole hab.

## The Nacre Assembly

Nacre's elected coordinators fund the Turnward and negotiate contact. Convenor Jalen Mora and a
small emergency committee suppressed an engineering forecast that predicted possible anchor
failures. They feared an uncontrolled exodus and believed local crews could repair the system.
Their secrecy gives the invaders real evidence without making occupation legitimate.

## The Safekeeping

The Safekeeping is a mobile society descended from people who escaped several failed habitats.
Its founding principle is that a home can be replaced and a person cannot. Its fleets map unstable
habitats, offer passage, and retain the capacity to evacuate populations quickly.

Repeated rescue failures hardened a compassionate mandate into doctrine. When leaders conclude
that residents cannot meaningfully consent under local pressure, they declare emergency custody,
secure transit nodes, and remove people anyway. Nacre's coming Turn resembles the early signals of
a disaster in their institutional memory. Commander Ossa Renn believes delay will kill millions.

The Safekeeping is the primary enemy force. Its retrieval crews use restraint weapons, drones,
portable cover, survey tools, and devices that lock local geometry. Individual personnel can
surrender, disagree, or assist the player. The institution remains responsible for coercion.

## The Departure Circle

The Departure Circle is a loose Nacre movement advocating the right and practical means to leave.
It predates contact with the Safekeeping. Some members collaborate because outside ships make a
long-denied option possible; others resist when voluntary passage becomes detention. The Circle
prevents the story from equating love of home with compulsory staying.

## The conflict

The Safekeeping plans to freeze Nacre into one stable adjacency long enough to evacuate everyone.
Its model correctly identifies damaged anchors but incorrectly treats the Long Turn itself as a
failure cascade. Permanent stabilization would sever water, cultivation, and heat relationships
that only exist across Turn states, making evacuation appear necessary by destroying the conditions
for staying. The player must stop the lock, expose Nacre's concealed risks, and make voluntary
passage possible before the Turn closes.
