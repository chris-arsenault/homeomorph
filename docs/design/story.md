# Story

## Dramatic question

Can the people of Nacre preserve the right to choose their future when their own government hid a
real danger and an outside rescuer has decided that consent is too risky to honor?

## Act I — Receiving

Turnward Crew Seven prepares for an ordinary Long Turn. A Safekeeping survey ship makes contact
and offers technical aid. Its data exposes damaged anchors that the Nacre Assembly knew about.
Before the crew can establish what the danger means, Commander Ossa Renn declares emergency
custody, occupies Receiving, and begins compulsory registration for departure.

The act establishes three truths simultaneously: Nacre is a functioning home, the Assembly lied,
and the Safekeeping's invasion is grounded in a sincere but incomplete diagnosis.

## Act II — Custody

The crew crosses occupied districts to reconnect local services, protect both people staying and
people choosing passage, and recover the Quiet Map: generations of transition records omitted from
the visitors' model. Safekeeping retrieval officer Nera Quill witnesses her command classify local
evidence as contamination and defects to the crew.

The Quiet Map shows that most transformations remain survivable, but three anchors will fail unless
repaired during the Turn. It also shows that the Safekeeping's stabilizers are creating the cascade
they predict. The crew publishes both the Assembly's concealment and the invaders' error. Neither
side can retain a simple account of itself.

## Act III — The Turn

As Nacre changes, the crew protects an open civic assembly and wins enough Safekeeping personnel
away from immediate stabilization to create a narrow alternative. In the final mission the player
coordinates three tasks: repair the failing anchors, disable the central lock, and keep a voluntary
departure corridor open. No single task can be abandoned without human cost.

The crew succeeds because locals, defectors, outside engineers, and voluntary evacuees each hold a
different part of the solution. Nacre completes the Long Turn damaged but viable. Some residents
leave. Some Safekeeping personnel remain to answer for their actions or help repair them. Commander
Renn can stand down, be relieved by her own officers, or force the crew to disable her command rig,
depending on accumulated mission outcomes.

## Choice model

The campaign has four tracked positions, expressed through actions rather than dialogue meters:

- **Open passage:** protect voluntary departure and do not obstruct non-coercive transports.
- **Local truth:** recover and publish evidence of the Assembly's concealment.
- **Mercy:** accept surrenders and avoid optional harm to trapped retrieval crews.
- **Shared repair:** preserve tools and personnel capable of repairing Nacre during the finale.

These flags change assistance, scenes, final mission options, and epilogue testimony. They never
remove a required mission. There is no single morality total and no perfect ending unlocked by
collecting enough virtue.

## Character resolutions

- Mara accepts public responsibility for enforcing an emergency decision she privately doubted.
- Iven chooses whether to join a survey voyage after helping make departure genuinely voluntary.
- Sela establishes a permanent independent passage office or joins the first outbound medical ship.
- Tarn either rebuilds civic anchors under open oversight or creates small neighborhood systems that
  cannot be hidden behind one authority.
- Nera testifies against emergency custody doctrine and chooses exile, Nacre residency, or return to
  organize reform depending on whom the player trusted.

The ending is neither restoration nor utopia. Nacre survives in a new shape, with open records,
open passage, and relationships that make future coercion harder.
